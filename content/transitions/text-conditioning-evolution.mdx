## Two different questions that get conflated

"How is a generator conditioned on text" is really two independent questions, and most confusion about
this design space comes from answering one while thinking about the other.

1. **Mechanism** — *where in the network does the condition enter, and what gets attended to what?*
   Cross-attention, [joint/shared self-attention](/concepts/joint-attention), [adaLN modulation](/concepts/adaln-modulation),
   sequence concatenation, and the [query bridge](/concepts/query-bridge) are five different answers.
2. **Source** — *what produces the embedding being fed in?* A [CLIP text encoder](/concepts/clip-text-encoder),
   a [frozen T5](/concepts/t5-text-encoder), a full vision-language model, or a set of
   [learnable queries](/concepts/query-bridge) run through an MLLM are different answers to this
   separate question, and — as the MetaQuery section below shows precisely — a source-side innovation
   can be completely agnostic to which mechanism receives it.

## 2022: cross-attention into a frozen-shape UNet

[Latent Diffusion Models](/papers/ldm-2022) states the mechanism plainly: "by introducing cross-attention
layers into the model architecture, we turn diffusion models into powerful and flexible generators for
general conditioning inputs such as text or bounding boxes." Concretely: the UNet's own spatial feature
map supplies queries; a separately encoded context sequence (CLIP text tokens, in the Stable Diffusion
implementations built on this paper) supplies keys and values. No parameters are added to the UNet's own
processing of the image beyond the cross-attention layers themselves — the image pathway and the text
pathway stay architecturally separate the entire time, meeting only at these dedicated junctions. That
separation is the mechanism's whole appeal: text conditioning is modular and cheap to add to an existing
image model, and it is exactly why cross-attention could also carry [spatial control signals](/concepts/adapter-conditioning)
(ControlNet, T2I-Adapter) and [image prompts](/concepts/decoupled-cross-attention) (IP-Adapter) a year
later without touching the base model's weights.

## 2022-2023: a second, unrelated mechanism for scalar conditioning

When [DiT](/papers/dit-2023) replaced the UNet with a plain transformer, it needed to inject the
timestep and (for class-conditional ImageNet) the class label — conditioning that is a single vector per
image, not a token sequence. DiT's ablation compares in-context token conditioning, cross-attention, and
[adaLN-Zero](/concepts/adaln-modulation) for this job, and adopts adaLN-Zero: regress a per-channel
scale, shift, and a zero-initialized gate from the fused timestep+class embedding, and apply it before
every attention and MLP block. This is not a competitor to cross-attention for *text* conditioning — it
answers a different sub-problem (single global vector, not a variable-length sequence) — but the two
mechanisms coexisted uneasily once systems needed both, which is part of what joint attention resolved.

## 2022-2025: the text-encoder-source ladder, independent of mechanism

[Imagen](/papers/imagen-2022) made the source question its own axis: swapping a [frozen T5](/concepts/t5-text-encoder)
in for CLIP's text tower, finding that "increasing the size of the language model... boosts both sample
fidelity and image-text alignment much more than increasing the size of the image diffusion model." SD3
then used *both* — two CLIP encoders and T5-XXL, concatenated — before [Qwen-Image](/papers/qwen-image-2025)
replaced the whole stack with a single vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL), motivated by a very specific
failure mode: complex text rendering inside generated images needs an encoder that already understands
layout and instructions, not just semantics. FLUX.2 is reported to use a Mistral LLM as its text encoder,
continuing the trend, but the atlas has not found or verified an official technical report for this claim
and states it here only as reported, not confirmed (see `data/systems/flux.yaml`).

## 2024: SD3 replaces cross-attention itself with joint attention

[SD3](/papers/sd3-2024) is the mechanism transition, not just a scaling paper. Its own text: "since text
and image embeddings are conceptually quite different, we use two separate sets of weights for the two
modalities... equivalent to having two independent transformers for each modality, but joining the
sequences of the two modalities for the attention operation, such that both representations can work in
their own space yet take the other one into account." Crucially, the paper ran the head-to-head
comparison this whole design space usually lacks: its own ablation reports that this [MM-DiT](/concepts/mmdit)
design "significantly outperforms the cross-attention and vanilla variants" it was tested against — the
clearest internal, paper-reported evidence in this atlas that joint attention beats cross-attention for
production-scale text-to-image, at the cost of full self-attention over the combined sequence instead of
a cheaper dedicated cross-attention module.

[Lumina-Image 2.0](/papers/lumina2-2025) then dropped the "two separate sets of weights" part of MM-DiT:
its [single-stream](/concepts/single-stream-dit) "Unified Next-DiT" treats text and image tokens as one
joint sequence through *one* shared set of weights, not two. This is the architecture
[SVG-T2I](/papers/svg-t2i-2025) later adopts wholesale for RAE-family generation — see below.

## 2023-2024: the adapter era for image and control conditioning, then its challenge

In parallel, a separate lineage answered a separate question — how to add a *new* condition (an edge
map, a face, an image prompt) to an *already-trained* model without retraining it. See
[Adapter-based conditioning](/lines/line-adapter-conditioning) for the full arc from
[ControlNet](/papers/controlnet-2023) through [IP-Adapter](/papers/ip-adapter-2023)'s
[decoupled cross-attention](/concepts/decoupled-cross-attention) to identity-preserving methods. The
challenge to this whole family arrived from inside the DiT era: [OminiControl](/papers/ominicontrol-2024)
showed a DiT's own frozen weights, reused for the condition image and concatenated into the same
sequence, could match specialized adapters for about 0.1% additional parameters — the same
[sequence-concatenation](/concepts/sequence-concat) idea SD3 used for text, now applied to a reference
image instead. [FLUX.1 Kontext](/papers/kontext-2025) scaled this specifically for editing seven months
later, and it is now the open reference point that line documents.

## 2024-2025: conditioning disappears into the architecture

[Chameleon](/papers/chameleon-2024) and [Emu3](/papers/emu3-2024) remove the mechanism question rather
than answer it: text and image are the same token vocabulary in the same autoregressive sequence from
pretraining onward, so an "earlier" image or caption is conditioning simply by being earlier in the
sequence a decoder-only transformer already predicts next-tokens over. There is no separate conditioning
module to name. [HunyuanImage 3.0](/papers/hunyuanimage3-2025) and [Janus-Pro](/papers/janus-pro-2025)
are 2025 instances of this same [native-token conditioning](/concepts/native-token-conditioning) idea at
larger scale.

## 2025: MetaQuery separates the source question from the mechanism question, formally

[MetaQueries](/papers/metaquery-2025) is easy to mis-file as "a new conditioning mechanism" because it is
so often discussed alongside cross-attention and joint attention. It is not one. Its own description of
the interface: a trainable connector "projects [query embeddings] to align with the input space of
text-to-image diffusion models... these models can be arbitrary as long as they have a conditional input
interface; we simply replace its original condition with our [query embeddings]." MetaQuery's actual
contribution is upstream — a fixed-size set of [learnable query tokens](/concepts/query-bridge), prepended
to the prompt and processed jointly by a frozen multimodal LLM, produces the conditioning *source*
without ever fine-tuning that LLM (preserving its understanding ability, the paper's explicit motivation).
What mechanism receives that source downstream is left to whatever diffusion decoder is plugged in —
cross-attention, joint attention, or otherwise. [BLIP3-o](/papers/blip3o-2025) follows the same pattern
independently, within weeks.

## 2025-2026: two different answers inside the RAE line, at once

This is where the field's current lack of convergence is most visible. Both published text-to-image
extensions of the frozen-foundation-encoder ("RAE-family") line picked *existing* mechanisms from
elsewhere in this narrative, not a new one built for the semantic latent itself — and they picked
different ones:

- **[SVG-T2I](/papers/svg-t2i-2025)** uses Lumina-Image 2.0's single-stream Unified Next-DiT: a Gemma2-2B
  text encoder, tokens concatenated with image tokens into one joint sequence, one shared set of
  transformer weights. This is the joint-attention branch of the narrative, unmodified, transplanted
  onto a frozen-DINOv3-derived latent instead of a VAE latent.
- **[Scale-RAE](/papers/scale-rae-2026)** states directly that it "adopts the MetaQuery architecture":
  256 learnable queries, a Qwen-2.5 1.5B LLM, a 2-layer MLP connector into a LightningDiT backbone. This
  is the query-bridge branch of the narrative, also unmodified, transplanted onto a frozen-SigLIP-2
  latent.

So the user intuition that motivated this whole survey — "in the RAE field it was like the MetaQuery
framework" — is correct for *one* of the two published RAE-family text-to-image systems, and wrong for
the other, which instead used joint attention. No paper in this atlas has directly compared the two
mechanisms on a matched RAE-family backbone, or asked whether a high-dimensional, anisotropic semantic
latent needs a different amount or kind of conditioning than a compact VAE latent does — see
[Conditioning mechanism choice in semantic latents](/problems/conditioning-mechanism-in-semantic-latent),
which this atlas records as an open problem rather than a solved one.

## What forced each move, in one line each

- UNet &rarr; DiT/adaLN: a transformer backbone made regressing scale/shift from a fused embedding a
  natural fit for *scalar* conditioning (timestep, class); it does not compete with cross-attention,
  which stayed the tool for *sequence-shaped* conditioning (text).
- Cross-attention &rarr; joint/MM-DiT attention: SD3's own ablation reported joint attention
  significantly outperforming both a cross-attention and a "vanilla" variant at production text-to-image
  scale — the clearest paper-internal evidence this survey found for any single mechanism transition.
- CLIP &rarr; T5 &rarr; T5+CLIP &rarr; full VLM as the encoder: each step chased a specific measured gap
  — Imagen found text-encoder scale mattered more than image-model scale; SD3 found combining encoders
  measurably helped; Qwen-Image needed an encoder that understood instructions and layout well enough to
  render text correctly inside images, which semantic-only embeddings could not supply.
- Adapters &rarr; sequence concatenation: adapters need one trained module per task, and can fight a
  frozen model's existing text conditioning (the problem IP-Adapter's decoupled cross-attention was
  built specifically to solve); OminiControl showed a sufficiently capable DiT absorbs a reference image
  as plain context tokens for a small fraction of an adapter's added parameters.
- Cross/joint attention &rarr; native-token conditioning: unified models removed the conditioning
  pathway as a design choice at all, motivated by wanting one training loop and one set of weights that
  transfers reasoning ability between understanding and generation, rather than by any measured
  weakness in cross- or joint attention specifically.
- Cross/joint attention &rarr; query bridge: MetaQuery's motivation is explicit and different again — not
  "make conditioning cheaper" but "upgrade the conditioning source to a full MLLM without damaging that
  MLLM's own understanding ability," which ruling out end-to-end fine-tuning made necessary.

The throughline is that "how text reaches the generator" has never been one design decision converging on
one answer. By 2026 it is at least three coexisting, actively used mechanism families (joint attention,
query bridge, native-token) sitting on top of an independently evolving source ladder (CLIP &rarr; T5
&rarr; VLM/LLM) — and the newest representation family in this atlas, RAE-style semantic latents, has
inherited that same unresolved plurality rather than settling it.
